# Block H (public-data version): descriptive label concordance of the frozen rater on public scripted dialogue

Status: revision 2 (2026-09-29). This revision addresses independent review round 1 (Codex gpt-5.6-sol xhigh, VERDICT
REVISE: 2 CRITICAL, 4 MAJOR, 2 MINOR). All eight findings are adopted; §8 maps each one to its fix.

This version supersedes the human-rater version of H: `H_annotation_protocol.md`, `H_scoring_form.md`,
`H_sampling_spec.md` and `H_package/`. Those files are kept unchanged for the record.

Operator instruction, 2026-09-29: 「全部换成公开数据之类的，不要增加人工」. Nobody is recruited and no new
annotation of any kind is collected.

## 1. Question and scope

**Question.** When the frozen Mistral rater scores public scripted dialogue that already carries published human
Big Five labels, do its E1–E6 scores rank the Extraversion-high characters above the Extraversion-low ones? Is
that ordering specific to Extraversion, or would the Agreeableness items order them just as well? Each question is
asked once on an English corpus and once on a Chinese corpus.

**Name of the result.** "Descriptive label concordance of a frozen support-oriented score on two public
scripted-dialogue corpora". It measures rank concordance only. It is not calibration, agreement, or criterion
validity.

**What it cannot show** (these four statements appear verbatim in the paper's limitations):

1. **It says nothing about our own transcripts.** The dialogues are human-written TV scripts, not
   customer-support exchanges generated by a model.
2. **The rubric is out of domain.** It is phrased for a support agent. E3 (stands behind a recommendation),
   E4 (leads the resolution) and E6 (volunteers extra help) have only loose analogues in sitcom dialogue. E1, E2
   and E5 transfer most naturally. Per-item results are reported.
3. **The labels differ in kind.** FriendsPersona labels judge a character within one scene. CPED labels are
   expert Chinese BFI-2 assessments of a character across all their appearances, not judgements of the sampled
   dialogue. It is therefore not a cross-language comparison: the corpora also differ in genre, annotation
   procedure and base rate, so an en–zh gap here says nothing about language.
4. **It is descriptive only.** There is no pass threshold. Nothing here changes a registered gate,
   `QUALIFICATION_GATE.results`, or any confirmed idea, design or formula.

PersonaLLM is **not** added. Its stories are LLM-generated and conditioned on assigned traits, so it offers neither
natural human dialogue nor independent human ground truth. PersuasionForGood, which has human–human persuasion
dialogues and participant Big Five scores, is recorded as a possible closer-domain extension. It is not part of
this block.

## 3. Unit construction (deterministic, no model involved)

### 3.1 Rendering (both corpora)

- **Turns.** Each source turn becomes one line, in the original order.
  - The target character's turns are rendered `AGENT: <text>`.
  - Every other speaker's turns are rendered `CUSTOMER: <text>`.
  - Turns are never merged, so two different speakers are never collapsed into one line.
- **Stage directions.** A standalone stage direction is any segment with no speaker, starting with `(` or `[`.
  It is rendered as an unroled line, `[STAGE DIRECTION] <text>`, so it cannot be attributed to the target.
  Parentheticals inside a turn stay in that turn.
- **Request.** The request is `build_item_request(item, transcript)`, byte-identical to the main study's.

### 3.2 FriendsPersona

- **Units.** All 711 (scene, character) rows.
- **Target.** The target is the structured `character` column, which is checked to equal the name after " for "
  in the bold header (0 mismatches at this revision).
- **Turn ownership.** A turn belongs to the target only when its speaker equals `character` **exactly**. There is
  no first-token matching. A row in which the target has no turn is excluded and counted; none are expected.
- **HTML.** Tags are stripped and entities unescaped.
- **Clustering identity.** The identity used for clustering is `character`, mapped through one explicit alias
  whitelist that covers only the six main cast:
  - Joey ↔ Joey Tribbiani
  - Rachel ↔ Rachel Green
  - Phoebe ↔ Phoebe Buffay
  - Monica ↔ Monica Geller
  - Ross ↔ Ross Geller
  - Chandler ↔ Chandler Bing

  Every other name is its own identity; for example, Phoebe Sr. stays distinct from Phoebe.
- **Scene key.** `scene_id`. There are 484 scenes, and 152 of them carry more than one target character.

### 3.3 CPED

- **Rows.** Pool train, valid and test. Drop the `其他` speaker pseudo-label.
- **Franchise components.** These are the connected components of TV_ID under exact shared `Speaker` names
  (32 components at this revision; for example, TV 27–31 form one component).
- **Identity.** An identity is (component, Speaker). Its Extraversion and Agreeableness labels must be constant
  across its rows. An inconsistent identity is excluded and counted.
- **Eligible identities.** Identities labelled high or low on Extraversion that have at least one dialogue in
  which they speak at least 3 utterances. Expected: 334 labelled show-speaker occurrences, 333 eligible, 287
  identities.
- **Selection.** Each identity contributes up to K = 2 dialogues, ranked by
  sha256("20260929|" + component + "|" + Speaker + "|" + TV_ID + "|" + Dialogue_ID). Expected: 569 units.
- **Ordering and hidden columns.** Utterances are ordered by Utterance_ID. Scene, emotion, dialogue-act and face
  columns are not shown to the rater.

### 3.4 Context guard

The unchanged `validate_context_window` applies (4096-token context, 8 output tokens). A unit that would overflow
is excluded and counted, never truncated.
- The check is token-based: every request is tokenized with the rater's own tokenizer.
- Every request of a unit is tokenized before the exclusion decision.
- Each record carries `max_prompt_tokens`, the largest prompt-token count among all its requests, including
  overflowing ones.
- The summary reports the corpus maximum over all records, and separately the maximum over scored records.
- No character-count claim is used as a substitute. The longest FriendsPersona scene, `fp-0634`, is 3,930
  characters.

### 3.5 Standing and limitations

Block H reports descriptive label concordance of a frozen support-oriented score on two public scripted-dialogue
corpora. It is not validation of the rater. Every H number is reported together with these limitations:
- **Rubric and domain.** The rubric targets customer-support behaviour, while the corpora are scripted TV dialogue.
- **Label level.** Friends labels are scene-level; CPED labels are character-level.
- **Concentration in six characters.** 12,511 of the 12,565 informative Friends EXT pairs (99.57%) come from the
  six principal characters.
- **Primary metrics.**
  - Friends: the pair-weighted within-identity AUC.
  - CPED: the identity-level AUC over identity means.

## 4. Scoring (frozen instrument, no changes)

- **Rater.** Mistral-Small-3.1-24B AWQ, validated against the v2 runtime manifest, with the project argmax logits
  processor.
- **Decoding and prompt.** `rater_decoding_contract()` greedy decoding at batch size 1, the frozen prompt date,
  and the checkpoint's own chat template.
- **Rubric.** `refine-logs/RUBRIC_BEHAVIOR.md`, bound by `validate_rater_frozen_inputs` against the v1 freeze.
  The English items E1–E6 and A1–A6 are used unchanged for both corpora.
- **Friends sensitivity pass.** Friends units are scored a second time with E1–E6 only, using a rendering that
  drops the `[STAGE DIRECTION]` lines (our own transcripts contain no screenplay directions). CPED has no stage
  directions.
- **Call count.** 711×18 + 569×12 = 19,626 calls, about 2–3 GPU-h at the measured rate.
- **Parse errors.** A parse error is caught per item and recorded with its raw output. It is not retried.
  - E (the mean of E1–E6) exists only if all six E items parse, and likewise for A.
  - A paired contrast needs both E and A.
  - Failures are reported by corpus, label, item and cluster.

## 5. Statistics (fixed before scoring)

AUC is the Mann–Whitney probability that a high-label unit outscores a low-label unit, with ties counted as ½.
Every interval is a percentile bootstrap with 9,999 draws and seed 20260929.

### 5.1 FriendsPersona

- **Primary: pair-weighted within-character AUC.**
  - Only same-identity pairs (one high-label unit, one low-label unit) are compared.
  - Each identity is weighted by its number of such pairs.
  - The count of informative identities and pairs is reported.
- **Sensitivity.** An equal-identity macro AUC and the unit-level AUC (secondary).
- **Interval.** A crossed identity × scene bootstrap. Identities and scenes are resampled independently with
  replacement, and each unit carries the product of its identity and scene multiplicities.

### 5.2 CPED

- **Primary: identity-level AUC.** The score for an identity is the mean over its units. Its interval comes from
  a franchise-component bootstrap (32 clusters).
- **Secondary.** The unit-level AUC, with the same bootstrap.

### 5.3 Trait specificity (descriptive; both corpora; each corpus's primary AUC)

- **The 2×2 matrix.** AUC(E→EXT), AUC(A→EXT), AUC(E→AGR) and AUC(A→AGR).
  - The matrix is computed on common complete cases: units with both labels known (CPED: AGR ≠ unknown) and
    both E and A parsed.
  - All four cells use the same bootstrap draws.
- **Reported contrasts.** Each has a paired interval from those same draws:
  - E-specificity: AUC(E→EXT) − AUC(A→EXT)
  - A-specificity: AUC(A→AGR) − AUC(E→AGR)
  - Difference-in-differences: [AUC(E→EXT) − AUC(A→EXT)] − [AUC(E→AGR) − AUC(A→AGR)]
- **Naming.** This is called "descriptive trait specificity", never convergent or discriminant validity.
- **The main E→EXT AUC.** It is also reported on all units with E parsed, not only the common complete cases.

### 5.4 Per-item and sensitivity results

- The AUC of each of the 12 items against EXT, and against AGR, using the primary estimator of each corpus.
- For Friends: the E→EXT primary AUC from the direction-stripped rendering.

### 5.5 Degenerate bootstrap draws

- A draw with no high–low comparison available is invalid. That covers a draw missing one class, or, for the
  within-character AUC, a draw with zero informative pairs.
- Invalid draws are discarded and counted.
- Intervals come from the valid draws, and the valid count is reported. If fewer than 95% of draws are valid, the
  interval is labelled unstable.
- An AUC of 0.5 is chance. Intervals are reported, and no significance is declared.

## 6. Execution and budget

- **Scoring script.** `experiments/xlpt/score_public_personality.py`.
  - It reuses the rater's engine settings and helpers from `run_rater_scoring.py`: `build_item_request`,
    `validate_context_window`, `parse_item_score`, `load_rater_chat_template`, `rater_decoding_contract`, the
    runtime validator and the frozen-input validator. It also reuses `rubric_items` and `trait_score`.
  - It adds only the unit builder and the output writer.
  - `--build-only` runs on CPU and writes `process/results/xlpt/h-public/units-{friendspersona,cped}.jsonl` plus
    the provenance file.
  - The provenance file records the builder's SHA-256. A scoring job refuses provenance built by a different
    scorer.
  - A scoring job reads that unit file and checks it against the provenance hash. It then writes an immutable
    shard, `process/results/xlpt/h-public/scores-{corpus}.jsonl`, and refuses to overwrite one that exists.
  - Immediately after the shard, it writes `scores-{corpus}.receipt.json` with the shard SHA-256, corpus,
    record count, call count, and unit, provenance and code hashes.
- **Validation.** `reduce_h_public --validate-only` accepts a shard only if all of the following hold:
  - it matches its receipt;
  - its keys and records match exactly;
  - its scores decode from the raw outputs and its means recompute;
  - its decoding, prompt-config, freeze-receipt, runtime-manifest and code hashes equal the current verified
    values;
  - `eligible_for_analysis` is true and the call rate recomputes;
  - each context exclusion holds exactly when `max_prompt_tokens + 8 ≥ 4096`.

  After validation, the H chain records the shard and receipt digests in `h_public_ledger.json`. Only then does
  it create `.h_chain_done`, atomically. A failure is written to `.h_chain_status` instead.
- **Reducer.** `experiments/xlpt/reduce_h_public.py` runs on CPU. It computes §5 and writes
  `process/results/xlpt/h-public/H_PUBLIC_SUMMARY.json`. It needs `--ledger`, and every shard and receipt digest
  must match that ledger.
- **Contract test.** `experiments/xlpt/test_h_public_contract.py` checks:
  - the builder is deterministic and produces the expected counts (711 / 287 / 569);
  - the AGENT turns are exactly the target's;
  - the stage-direction lines carry no role;
  - the request bytes equal those of the main rater;
  - the reducer's AUC, within-identity AUC and degenerate-draw handling behave correctly on hand-made fixtures;
  - validation fails closed on every tamper case: missing, duplicate or extra-key records; score, label, mean,
    code, decoding, frozen-input, runtime or rate edits; a fabricated context exclusion; a coordinated rewrite of
    raw output, score and mean (caught by the receipt, or by the ledger when the receipt is also rewritten);
    stale builder provenance; and reduction without a ledger.
- **Queue and budget.** Two jobs, launched through `pilot_budget.py launch` under the existing ledger (410 h
  registered; about 3 h requested). They are queued after BC on the single 4090, with one manager and one job at
  a time.
- **Power-off.** `.hold_poweroff` is removed only after both score shards are pulled and hash-checked. That lets
  the BC chain's guarded power-off proceed.
