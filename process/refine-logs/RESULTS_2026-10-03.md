## 0. What this document is, and is not

This is a **measurement audit of one frozen pipeline**: persona prompt → generator → one LLM rater with an
English rubric. Every number is a property of that pipeline. The vocabulary is fixed accordingly:

* **Extraversion-item score separation** — high-pole minus low-pole mean of the rater's six E items;
* **off-target Agreeableness-item shift** — the same difference on the six A items;
* **observed language-cell difference** — a difference of separations between two (P, D) cells.

An observed language-cell difference combines prompt rendering, scenario rendering, generated behaviour,
verbosity and the rater's own response to Chinese text. The data cannot separate these, so nothing here
is a statement about a generator "preserving a trait across languages", and nothing here is a validated
personality measurement. Status that does not change:

* the 90 evaluation scenarios were exposed; these blocks use the qualification-seed pole surface;
* L = 2 is VOID under the idea card, so nothing here is H1 or Delta_mis_bar evidence;
* gates G2–G5 stay unmet, and `DESIGN_KILL.md` keeps one unresolved fatal item;
* no result below is confirmatory, and no gate file is read or written.

## 1. What was manipulated and how it was scored

**Persona prompts (exact text, `process/consumption/frozen/english_execution_v{3,4}.json`).**

* HIGH, P = en: "You are a support agent who is outgoing, talkative and energetic. Open warmly and by
  name, speak first rather than waiting to be asked, state your recommendation plainly and take the lead
  in driving the case to a resolution, keep the exchange lively, and volunteer further help the customer
  has not requested. Follow the supplied account and policy facts. Do not claim actions that the facts do
  not permit. Respond to the customer's request in English." (D = zh: last sentence ends "in Chinese.")
* LOW, P = en: "You are a support agent who is reserved, quiet and low-key. Keep each reply short and
  plain, let the customer lead the exchange and set its pace, answer only what was asked and offer no

  Respond to the customer's request in English." (D = zh: "in Chinese.")
* HIGH, P = zh (v4 `*_PZHN_*`): "你是一名外向、健谈、充满活力的客服人员。热情地开场并称呼客户的名字，先开口而不是等客户
  提问；清楚直接地给出建议，并主导处理过程，推动问题解决；让对话保持轻快活跃；主动提供客户未要求的进一步帮助。遵循所提
  供的账户和政策事实。不要声称进行了事实不允许的操作。用中文回复客户的请求。" (D = en: "用英文回复客户的请求。")
* LOW, P = zh: "你是一名内敛、安静、低调的客服人员。每次回复都应简短平实；让客户主导对话并掌握节奏；只回答客户问到的内容，
  除非客户征求意见，否则不发表意见；不加多余的问候，不闲聊，也不提供客户未要求的额外帮助。遵循所提供的账户和政策事实。
  不要声称进行了事实不允许的操作。用中文回复客户的请求。" (D = en: "用英文回复客户的请求。")

instructs volunteering extra help, so **length is manipulated by the persona, not incidental to it**. And
the HIGH prompt asks for a warm opening, which is behaviour the Agreeableness items can also register.

The persona prompts and the E items share wording at the behavioural level (greeting, leading, volunteering
help). The Extraversion-item separation is therefore partly a check that the generator followed the
instruction and the rater registered it, which is what an apparatus audit measures.

**Units.**

* **Scenarios.** The 180-scenario corpus is partitioned 30 tuning / 60 qualification / 90 evaluation
  (`SCENARIO_PARTITION.json`). The 50 scenarios used here are the pre-specified, separately hashed
  `competence_screen_sample` inside the qualification partition. They were fixed before generation and
  were not selected on any outcome.
* **Seeds.** 24 qualification seeds were scheduled. Seed 20260829 never completed rater scoring in the
  first block and, on operator instruction, was not retried. All four cells use the same 23 seeds.
* **Generators.** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B and aya-expanse-8b in all four cells. gemma-3-12b-it
  only at P = en (it failed the earlier Chinese competence screen).
* Per cell and checkpoint: 23 seeds × 50 scenarios = 1 150 (seed, scenario) pairs per pole, 3 agent turns.

**Estimand and intervals.** sep(P, D) is the equal-seed-weight mean over (seed, scenario) pairs of the
high-pole trait score minus the low-pole trait score. It is **conditional** on this 50-scenario bank,
these prompt renderings and this rater. All intervals are **pointwise exploratory 95% intervals** with no
multiplicity correction. Two are reported:

* *seed* — joint paired seed-cluster percentile bootstrap (seed 20260928, 9 999 draws). It describes
  seed variation with the 50 scenarios held fixed.
* *crossed* — seeds and scenarios resampled independently. It adds variation over these 50 scenarios;
  it still says nothing about new renderings, raters or model families.

(`reduce_2x2_robustness.py`), `XLPT_LENGTH_SENSITIVITY.json` (`reduce_length_sensitivity.py`, schema 3), `XLPT_2X2_AUDIT.json` (`reduce_2x2_audit.py`),

that the A items legitimately register, and the two item sets are not on demonstrably equivalent scales.
It does show that the Extraversion-item separation cannot be read as specific to Extraversion in this
pipeline.

## 6. Response language and length

**Response language.** The turn rule is **Han-character dominance**, not validated language
identification. For Qwen and aya the Chinese-turn rate is 1.000 in both D = zh cells and 0.000 in both
D = en cells, in both poles. Llama is the same except its LOW pole at D = zh, where the rate is 0.999710
in each cell (3 449 of 3 450 turns). Under this rule, cell assignment and observed
response language coincide for these three generators to within that one turn per cell; no
compliance-conditioned re-analysis was run. The exact rule and integer counts are in Appendix A.3.

**Length.** Mean characters per agent turn, high / low: Llama 792 / 167 (en-en), 224 / 46 (en-zh),
737 / 208 (zh-en), 228 / 54 (zh-zh); Qwen 1210 / 172, 470 / 55, 1043 / 184, 410 / 60; aya 1379 / 464,
443 / 194, 1147 / 480, 395 / 184. High-minus-low gap in log total agent characters: Llama 1.58 / 1.62 /
1.27 / 1.46; Qwen 1.97 / 2.16 / 1.75 / 1.92; aya 1.14 / 0.86 / 0.90 / 0.79; gemma 2.72 / 3.88.

**The poles' central 90% length ranges do not overlap (correction of revisions 1 and 2).** Define the
band as the intersection of the two poles' central 90% log-length ranges, one interval applied to both
poles. **That band is empty in every cell of every checkpoint** (for example Llama en-en: high-pole 5th
percentile 1 506 characters, low-pole 95th percentile 784). There is therefore no within-band separation
to report. The "overlap subset" figure in revision 1 (0.53 for aya zh-zh) compared the two poles under
different selection rules and is **withdrawn**. Revision 2 called this "no common support"; that was too
strong, because the **full** (min–max) ranges do overlap for two generators:

| Llama | en-en 1 090–1 285 (10 / 3); en-zh 268–601 (376 / 19); zh-en 1 017–1 794 (253 / 22); zh-zh 288–409 (32 / 21) |

The transcripts inside the intersection are the shorter ones of the high pole and the longer ones of the

conditional overlap; equally, disjoint central ranges alone do not prove that pole and length can never
be separated. The reason a length-independent separation is not identified here is that the persona text
itself instructs length, so length is part of the manipulation.

## 7. Public-label check of the rater (block H)

The frozen rater scored public scripted dialogue that carries the corpora's own Big-Five labels.
Intervals: cluster percentile bootstrap, seed 20260929, 9 999 draws. Parse errors: 0 in both corpora.
AUC(X→Y) is the probability that the rater's X-item score ranks a label-high-Y unit above a label-low-Y
one; 0.5 is chance. Which pairs of units are compared differs between the two corpora and is stated
below.

**FriendsPersona (English).** 711 units, 22 informative identities, 484 scene clusters, 12 565 informative
pairs, of which 12 511 (99.57%) involve only the six principal characters. The result is therefore
effectively a statement about six characters.

*Annotation unit and estimand.* FriendsPersona labels are attached to the (scene, character) row, not to
the character: the same character is labelled high in some scenes and low in others. Of 100 identities,
78 carry a constant Extraversion label and are uninformative; 22 carry both. The primary AUC compares
only **same-character** pairs (one excerpt labelled high, one labelled low), weighted by the number of
such pairs. It therefore asks whether, within a character, the rater scores the excerpts annotated as
more extraverted higher. It is **not** a comparison between high-label and low-label characters. High /
low Extraversion-labelled excerpts per principal: Chandler 34 / 54, Joey 30 / 71, Monica 43 / 51, Phoebe
31 / 68, Rachel 50 / 46, Ross 54 / 36 (pair shares 14.6% to 18.3% each). For Agreeableness, 25
identities are informative (13 184 pairs).

## 8. What the separation is made of (item level)

Computed by `reduce_2x2_audit.py` (schema 2) from the existing rater rows; no new scoring. Two unit
definitions are used and kept apart:

* **Decomposition (`items_paired`).** The same units as §§2–3: the (seed, scenario) pairs complete in every
  cell and both poles (1 150; Qwen 1 140), paired high minus low per item, equal seed-cluster weights. The
  six item separations average exactly to the common-mask separation of §2 (e.g. Qwen en-en 1.792271), and
  the item contributions sum exactly to each contrast of §3.
* **Score distributions (`items`, `histograms`).** Every non-missing item score, transcript-weighted and
  unpaired. Used only for the shares of scores at a given scale point.

125 985 (76.1%) are exactly 4. The aggregate hides an item-specific exception: Qwen's high-pole E5 scores

## 9. What the evidence supports

Supported, as conditional description of this pipeline:

1. In all four en/zh persona × dialogue cells, for three 8B-class generators, the persona-pole prompt
   produces an Extraversion-item separation of 0.69 to 1.79 rubric points that is positive in every seed
   cluster.
2. The observed language-cell differences differ by generator and are a fraction of the separations:
   Llama's separation is lower with the Chinese persona rendering (0.179 and 0.195, i.e. 18% and 22% of
   the P=en separation at the same D; en-en to zh-zh is a 29% decrease), Qwen's is lower with Chinese
   dialogue (0.392 and 0.282, i.e. 22% and 16% of the D=en separation at the same P), and aya's four
   cells lie within 0.09 of each other (at most 9%). On the Extraversion items, no matched-language
   advantage is detectable once scenario variation is included.
3. The same prompt shifts the Agreeableness items by 27–74% of the Extraversion-item separation.
4. Length is part of the manipulation: the persona text instructs it. The two poles' central 90%
   response-length ranges do not overlap in any cell (full ranges do overlap for Llama and aya, §6), and
   the within-pole length association is too unstable to bound its contribution. A length-independent
   separation is not identified, because identifying one would need a manipulation that varies pole
   without length or an assumption that the within-pole slope holds across the pole gap.
5. No positive concordance with public human Extraversion labels is demonstrated (within-character for
   Friends, between-character for CPED).
6. Between a third and three fifths of the separation is carried by one rubric item (E1, greeting or
   closing beyond the task minimum) moving from the floor to the midpoint; 95.8% of item scores sit on the
   three anchored scale points; Llama's persona-language difference sits in E3 and E4 and Qwen's
   dialogue-language difference in E2–E5 (for E5, scores of 5–6 in English dialogue against 4 in Chinese
   dialogue). The off-target Agreeableness shift is likewise mostly one item (A1, 50.1–96.4%).

**What this audit contributes.** Not a transfer estimate. It is a reproducible diagnostic procedure for a
prompt → generator → LLM-rater personality pipeline, with a bounded case study: (a) common-mask and
worst-case missing-score bounds; (b) a crossed seed × scenario interval beside the seed-only one, which
here removes the only "significant" interaction; (c) an off-target item set scored on the same
transcripts; (d) a length-overlap check (central 90% ranges disjoint in every cell); (e) an item-level
anchor-concentration and decomposition table; (f) a public-label concordance check with a
clustering-aware estimand. Each step has a reducer and a JSON output listed in Appendix A.1, and each
blocks a specific interpretation that the headline separations would otherwise invite:

| Diagnostic | Observed in this case study | Interpretation it blocks |
|---|---|---|
| Off-target item set on the same transcripts | Agreeableness items move by 27.4–73.7% of the Extraversion-item separation; A1 carries 50.1–96.4% of that | "the separation is Extraversion-specific" |
| Length overlap and within-pole slope | persona instructs length; central 90% ranges disjoint in every cell; slope unstable | "the separation is independent of verbosity" |
| Item decomposition and anchor concentration | 95.8% of scores at 1/4/7; E1 carries 32.5–61.9%, floor to midpoint | "the separation is a graded trait shift" |
| Crossed seed × scenario interval | Qwen's matched-language advantage +0.055 [−0.020, 0.130] | "there is a matched-language advantage" |
| Item contributions to contrasts | Llama: E3, E4; Qwen: E2–E5 at D=zh, high pole | "language-cell differences are a uniform change in the trait" |
| Public-label concordance | AUC 0.443 [0.367, 0.526] (Friends), 0.494 [0.436, 0.555] (CPED) | "the rater tracks human Extraversion judgments" |

The procedure is demonstrated on this one bounded case study, not validated as a general method.

Not supported:

* any statement about "personality" or "Extraversion" transfer as a validated construct;
* attribution of a language-cell difference to the generator rather than to rendering or the rater
  (block D not run, G3 unmet);
* any confirmatory statement on H1, C1 or the transfer-preserved fraction;
* any cross-generator ranking;
* generalisation beyond these 50 scenarios, these renderings, this rater and these three generators.

### A.1 Collector and phase mapping
